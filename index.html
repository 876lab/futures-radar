Help

Start here

Futures Radar scans crypto futures on Binance, Gate and Bybit and describes what each chart shows in plain words, using fixed rules built from the same indicators as the Gate app: Bollinger bands, Supertrend, RSI and MACD. The reading is the same for everyone. It is a tool for reading charts and learning, not advice and not a prediction.

How the pages fit together

  1. News. Headlines and the economic calendar. An amber note appears on Setups and on each chart when a big US release (CPI, jobs, a Fed decision) is close, because prices often spike both ways then.
  2. Setups. Markets where the chart currently shows a long or short setup, highest agreement first. By default only confirmed setups are listed: at least one of the 4H and 1D trends points the same way and no delisting is known.
  3. A market's chart screen. The candle-size strip at the top shows the reading on 15m, 1H, 4H and 1D side by side; in the backtest, setups going against both bigger trends did worst. Below it, the chart reading card has the headline, the agreement score, the checklist, the confirmation box and, when there's enough history, how similar setups did in last night's backtest.
  4. Levels on the chart. Entry zone, stop and the two targets for a setup; Fibonacci levels showing how deep the current pullback is and the next levels each way; the order book showing whether the market is thin (thin books mean bigger spikes and worse stop fills).
  5. Position size calculator. Under a setup: your account size and risk % turn into a position size. With a max loss per trade set in Settings, it turns red when a plan would lose more than that at the stop.
  6. Simulator. Replays the app's rules on past prices, shows last night's backtest on the top Gate markets, and reads your own Gate history (Your trades): win rate by coin, side and time, and what the reading said when you opened.
  7. Starred. Tap the star on any row to keep a market in your own list.

The four readings

Long setup
Uptrend, the bigger timeframe agrees, and there's a fresh trigger or a pullback that held, without price being stretched. The entry zone, stop and targets are shown.
Short setup
The same, pointing down. A short gains when price falls.
Wait
A trend is in place, but there's no fresh trigger or price has run far from its average. The card shows a better-entry area and where the trend would break.
No setup
No clear direction, a choppy market, or the candle size and the bigger timeframe disagree.

Numbers worth knowing

Agreement (0–100)
How many indicators line up. 70 or more is strong agreement. It is not the chance of winning.
Past win rate
How often setups like this one reached target 1 in the nightly backtest, with a range. Shown only when there are at least 100 past trades.
R
The amount at risk on one trade (entry to stop). Target 1 is 1.5R, target 2 is 3R; +1R means a gain the size of the risk, −1R means the stop was hit.

Keep in mind

  • Readings update while a candle is still forming, so a setup can appear and disappear before the candle closes. 1H and 4H readings change less often than 15m.
  • Setups lose often. In the nightly backtests roughly 4 in 10 setups reached target 1, and the average result across all setups is close to break-even after fees. Results depend heavily on keeping each loss small and similar in size.
  • A single oversized loss can wipe out weeks of gains. The stop level and the max-loss setting exist for that reason.
  • Leverage magnifies losses as well as gains, and a position can be liquidated before the stop. See the Risk Disclosure.

How to read Futures Radar

Each row is one market. When the same coin trades on several exchanges, it appears once. Its price and 24-hour change come from the exchange with the most trading, and volume adds up all of them.

Getting around

Bottom bar
Markets, Setups, Simulator, News and Starred. Settings is the sliders button at the top right. The bar slides away while you scroll and comes back when you stop; switch that off in Settings to keep it on screen. On a computer the same buttons sit above the list.
Compact
Shows one line per market so more fit on screen.
Swipes on a chart
Swipe in from the left edge, or use your phone's back gesture, to return to the list. Swipe left or right on the chart reading to change candle size.
Pull to refresh
At the top of any page, pull down and let go to reload prices (and headlines on the News page). Same as the refresh button at the top.
Delisting tab
Markets that Binance, Gate or Bybit has scheduled for delisting, with the exchange and the date in your local time, soonest first. Those markets also get a red Delisting tag, and their chart screen shows the date. Exchanges usually close open positions at the delisting time; on Gate only closing trades are allowed from the reduce-only time.
Small line on each row
The price over the last 24 hours, green if it ended higher.

The chart reading

The app describes what the chart shows using fixed rules. It is the same for everyone and is not advice or an instruction to trade.

Long setup
The trend is up, the bigger timeframe agrees, and there's a fresh trigger (or a pullback that held) without price being stretched. The entry zone, stop and targets are shown.
Short setup
The same, pointing down. A short gains if price falls.
Wait
A trend is in place but there's no fresh trigger, or price has already run far from its average. The chart shows a "better entry" area and where the trend would break.
No setup
No clear trend, or the short and bigger timeframes disagree.
Stop loss
The level where the setup is proven wrong.
Targets
Target 1 is 1.5 times the risk, target 2 is 3 times. A common approach is to close half at target 1 and move the stop to entry.
Confirmed
A long or short setup is confirmed when at least one of the 4H and 1D trends points the same way and no delisting is scheduled or announced. In the first nightly backtest, setups that went against both bigger trends did worst. Liquidity and the 24-hour range are shown for context. Setups shows only confirmed setups unless you switch that off in Settings. Confirmed setups can still fail.
Your trades
On the Simulator page, load your position history exported from Gate (CSV). You see your win rate, average win vs average loss, biggest loss, and breakdowns by side, coin, time of day, weekday and holding time. Check what the chart said shows what the 1H and 4H chart reading was when you opened each of your latest 40 trades. The file is read on this device and never uploaded; Remove my trades deletes it.
Last night's backtest
Each night the app's own rules are replayed on the top 30 Gate markets (15m, 1H, 4H). The Simulator page shows how often confirmed and not-confirmed setups reached target 1, with a range for luck, and the chart reading shows the past win rate for setups like the one on screen. It needs at least 100 past trades before it shows a number. Past results don't guarantee future ones.
Crypto cycle gauge
On the News page (and a line on Setups): where Bitcoin sits in its roughly four-year cycle, from 0 (like past cycle lows) to 100 (like past cycle tops). It averages four price measures: price vs the 200-week average, the Mayer Multiple (price vs the 200-day average), the Pi Cycle lines and the distance from the all-time high, and shows the time since the last halving. Updated daily. It describes how today compares with past cycles; it doesn't predict the next move and doesn't change any setup.
Fibonacci levels
Lines on the chart and a card under it, from the last main move (a swing of at least 3× the average candle range). Retracements (23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%) show how much of the move price has given back; pullbacks in a trend often pause around 38.2–61.8%. Extensions (127.2%, 161.8%) are levels traders watch past the swing end after a breakout. They are zones, not exact prices, and don't change the chart reading. Switch them off with the Fibonacci chip above the chart.
Order book
On each chart: how much money is waiting to buy and to sell within 1% and 2% of the price, the spread, and unusually large orders. A thin book means bigger spikes and worse stop fills. Large orders often come from automated market makers and can move or vanish in a second, so they are not reliable support or resistance.
Candle-size strip
Above the chart reading, the reading on 15m, 1H, 4H and 1D side by side, so you can see at a glance whether the bigger picture agrees. Tap one to open it.
Agreement
How many indicators line up, from 0 to 100. Above 70 is strong agreement. It is not a probability of winning.
Position size calculator
Works out a position size from your own account size and risk %. A calculator using your own numbers, not a recommendation.
Big news warning
An amber note on the chart reading and Setups page when a big US release (like CPI, jobs or a Fed decision) is within 30 minutes. Prices often spike both ways then, and stops can fill worse than planned. Earlier in the day, Setups shows a quiet “Big news later today” line with the times so you can plan around them. A second switch also counts Japan, China and euro-area releases (like Bank of Japan or ECB rate decisions). It never changes the chart reading.
B and S on the chart
Where Supertrend flipped to buy (B) or sell (S).

The indicators

Supertrend (10, 3)
A trailing line under price in an uptrend (green) or above it in a downtrend (red). A close through it flips the direction.
Bollinger bands (20, 2)
A channel around the 20-candle average. Price near the top band is stretched, near the middle is a typical pullback, near the bottom is weak.
RSI 6 / 12 / 24
How hard price has been pushed over short, medium and longer spans. Above 70 is often overheated, below 30 oversold.
MACD (DIF, DEA)
Momentum. When DIF crosses above DEA and bars turn green, buyers are gaining. The reverse favours sellers.
EMA 20 / 50 / 200
Moving averages. Price above them, with the short line on top, is a classic uptrend.
Funding
A small fee swapped between longs and shorts every few hours on perpetuals. Positive means longs pay shorts.
Volume
Dollar value traded. A move on rising volume is more trustworthy.
News
Headlines from crypto and financial news sites, the Federal Reserve and the SEC, refreshed every 15 minutes. Fear & Greed sums up market mood from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed).
Live
The dot at the top shows how fresh prices are. "Live · now" means ticks are streaming in. "Delayed" means no tick for 30 seconds; the app reconnects on its own.
